The governing rule

History can test randomness. It cannot make a fair future combination “due”.

Frequency, recency, streaks and historical pair counts remain useful for data auditing and randomness diagnostics. They are deliberately excluded from ticket probability weights. The optimisation tools below focus on quantities that can be measured objectively.

Method & Guardrails

A hierarchy of claims

The site labels what is exact mathematics, what is simulation evidence, what is historical description, and what remains experimental.

01Exact

Combination probability

C(45,6) defines the standard-game Division 1 sample space. Distinct combinations add linearly within one draw.

02Exact

Subset coverage

Pair, triple and quadruple coverage is counted directly. Repeated subsets are measurable portfolio redundancy.

03Simulated

Portfolio outcomes

Monte Carlo estimates lower-division portfolio behaviour and reports Wilson 95% confidence intervals.

04Descriptive

Historical diagnostics

Frequency, pairs, entropy and z-scores inspect past variation but do not weight future tickets.

05Experimental

Anti-crowding

Human-choice research may inform conditional prize-sharing risk; the current heuristic is not Australia-calibrated.

06Guardrail

No prediction theatre

No “hot”, “cold”, “due”, pair-lift, recency or machine-learning score is presented as a next-draw probability edge.
