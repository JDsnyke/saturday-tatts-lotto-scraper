Σ
Exact any-prize algorithm

Count the six-ball draws where every ticket stays below three matches, then take the complement.

Each portfolio ticket is represented by a base-3 match counter with surviving states 0, 1 or 2. Any state that would give a ticket a third main-number match is discarded. Subtracting the surviving no-prize sets from C(45,6) gives the exact any-prize probability.

✓
Division 4 certificate

Pairwise overlap of at most one number globally maximises Division 4-or-better probability.

Two six-number games sharing at most one number cannot both match at least four of the six winning main numbers. Their ≥4-main events are disjoint, so the portfolio reaches the universal sum-of-marginals upper bound.

i
Interpretation

Probability-of-superiority is a benchmark statistic, not the probability a strategy wins the lottery.

It measures how often a randomly chosen portfolio from one generator scores better than one from another generator on the named benchmark metric.

Method

Exact mathematics first, simulation second

Exact unions, rigorous bounds, simulations and descriptive structure are labelled separately.

Exact

Any-prize portfolio union

Complement dynamic programming counts every six-main-number set in which no ticket reaches three matches.

Exact

Pair intersections

Two-ticket ≥3-main and ≥4-main event intersections are counted directly from shared, ticket-only and outside groups.

Certified

Bonferroni lower bound

S1 − S2 remains a rigorous lower bound and a cheap optimisation signal.

Optimal

Division 4+ certificate

If pairwise ≥4-main intersections are zero, the exact union reaches the universal upper bound.

Simulated

Shared draws

Monte Carlo remains for exploratory metrics that are not solved exactly; compared portfolios see the same simulated draw sample.

Rejected

No simulated-training optimiser

A direct sampled-training prototype overfit and failed to generalise, so it is not shipped as a recommended strategy.
