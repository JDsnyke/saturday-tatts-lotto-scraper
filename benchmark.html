How to read the benchmark

A positive lower-division effect is a portfolio result, not a forecasting edge.

Triple and quadruple coverage are exact structural measures. Prize-hit rates are Monte Carlo estimates. Bootstrap intervals describe variability across independently seeded portfolios conditional on the shared simulated draw sample.

i
Interpretation guardrail

Probability-of-superiority is not “chance this strategy wins the lottery”.

It is the empirical probability that a randomly chosen coverage portfolio scores better than a randomly chosen QuickPick portfolio on the named benchmark metric.

Method

What this improves over v2.1

The old reference comparison used one seeded portfolio per strategy. The new benchmark estimates distributions and strategy differences across many seeds.

01Exact

Structural coverage

Pair, triple and quadruple efficiency is counted directly for every portfolio.

02Simulated

Shared draws

Every portfolio sees the same draw sample, reducing irrelevant Monte Carlo noise in comparisons.

03Bootstrap

Mean difference interval

Portfolio distributions are resampled to estimate a 95% interval for the favourable mean difference.

04Effect

Probability-of-superiority

Pairwise distribution comparison shows how consistently one strategy beats the other on a metric.

05Invariant

Division 1 equality

Equal-size sets of distinct standard games always retain identical Division 1 probability.
